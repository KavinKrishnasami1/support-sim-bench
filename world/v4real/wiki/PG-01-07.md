# PG-01-07: IT Security Procedural Guide: Access Control (AC), Revision 8

## 1 Introduction

Access control is the process of granting or denying requests to use information, use information processing
services, and enter company facilities. This guide focuses on logical access controls in the Access Control (AC)
family of security controls. Physical access controls are covered in PG-12-64: Physical and Environmental
Protection. Personnel access controls are covered in the Common Control Catalog (CCC) and Quillmark's People
policies.

Every Quillmark system must follow the practices described in this guide. Any deviations from the security
requirements established in POL-100 must be coordinated by the appropriate system security owner and approved by
the CISO. Any deviations, exceptions, or other conditions not following company policies and standards must be
submitted using the Security Deviation Request form.

### 1.1 Purpose

This guide provides an overview of access control, roles and responsibilities, the access control requirements, and
the company-defined parameter settings for them. Company-defined parameter settings are shown in [brackets].

### 1.2 Policy

POL-100 states the company's IT security policy. The policy statements related to access control are reproduced in
Appendix B for ease of reference.

## 2 Roles and Responsibilities

2.1 **System Owners** are responsible for ensuring that accounts are created, enabled, modified, disabled and
removed in accordance with this guide and PG-01-01.

2.2 **Supervisors (managers) and contract sponsors** are responsible for requesting access for their staff based on
job function and need-to-know, and for requesting access termination for departing or transferring personnel
(see PG-03-23).

2.3 **Data Owners (group owners)** approve access to the data they own and review it (Section 3.2, AC-06(07)).

2.4 **IT Help (the desk)** carries out account actions on request, once the requirements of POL-100 are met.

## 3 Access Control Requirements

### 3.1 AC-02 Account Management

Control:
a. Define and document the types of accounts allowed and specifically prohibited for use within the system;
b. Assign account managers;
c. Require [manager approval, plus data-owner or Security approval as required by POL-100 Chapter 4] for
   requests to create accounts or grant group membership;
d. Specify authorized users of the system, group and role membership, and access authorizations;
e. Require approvals by [designated account managers as specified in AC-02.b] for requests to create accounts;
f. Create, enable, modify, disable, and remove accounts in accordance with [PG-01-01, Identification and
   Authentication, PG-01-07, Access Control, and company-defined procedures or conditions (as applicable)];
g. Monitor the use of accounts;
h. Notify account managers and [System Owner, system administrator, and/or system security owner] within:
   1. [14 days] when accounts are no longer required;
   2. [14 days] when users are terminated or transferred; and
   3. [14 days] when system usage or need-to-know changes for an individual;
i. Authorize access to the system based on:
   1. A valid access authorization;
   2. Intended system usage; and
   3. [Role privileges identified in the system's Types of Users table];
j. Review accounts for compliance with account management requirements [annually];
k. Establish and implement a process for changing shared or group account authenticators (if deployed) when
   individuals are removed from the group; and
l. Align account management processes with personnel termination and transfer processes.

Control Enhancements:

(02) Account Management | Automated Temporary and Emergency Account Management. Automatically [disable] temporary
and emergency accounts after [no more than 90 days].

(03) Account Management | Disable Accounts. Disable accounts within [30 days] when the accounts:
  (a) Have expired;
  (b) Are no longer associated with a user or individual;
  (c) Are in violation of organization policy; or
  (d) Have been inactive for [90 days].

(07) Account Management | Privileged User Accounts.
  (a) Establish and administer privileged user accounts in accordance with [a role-based access scheme];
  (b) Monitor privileged role or attribute assignments;
  (c) Monitor changes to roles or attributes; and
  (d) Revoke access when privileged role or attribute assignments are no longer appropriate.

(13) Account Management | Disable Accounts for High-Risk Individuals. Disable accounts of individuals within
[24 hours] of discovery of [an account compromise or a credible insider threat].

**Implementation guidance.**

For AC-02, an account must be reviewed, and modified, disabled or removed as needed, whenever a change is
required. For example, if an individual transfers, terminates, or their relationship to the company changes, the
individual's accounts must be reviewed and modifications, disablement, or revocation of their accounts made, as
appropriate, within the timeframes in AC-02.h. Similarly, when an individual leaves a group, any group account
authenticators must be changed to ensure that former group members do not retain access. PG-03-23: Termination and
Transfer contains information on handling personnel terminations and transfers.

For AC-02(02), systems must disable temporary and emergency accounts after no more than 90 days. For example, if
possible, any emergency or temporary account should be set to automatically expire in 90 days, otherwise a manual
process will have to be established.

For AC-02(03), user accounts that are no longer associated with a user, are expired, are in violation of company
policy, or have been inactive for 90 days must be disabled within 30 days. The automated inactivity sweep disables
inactive accounts and quarantines their devices (see GUI-LTL). If appropriate, restored access for disabled
accounts may be requested by the user. (See POL-100 5.3 for what re-enabling requires.)

For AC-02(07), systems must follow a role-based or attribute-based access scheme to establish and administer
privileged user accounts. A process must be established to monitor role and attribute assignments and changes to
them. Access must be revoked when privileged roles or attribute assignments are no longer needed. For example, when
an individual's role changes, the role assignment and/or attributes must reflect and change between the previous and
new role.

For AC-02(13), accounts of individuals who pose a significant risk must be disabled within 24 hours after it has been
discovered that there has been an incident related to an account compromise or a credible insider threat has been
identified. Accounts should only be disabled as directed by the CISO, the Incident Response Team, or POL-100
Chapter 9.

### 3.2 AC-05 Separation of Duties

Control:
a. Identify and document [duties that need separation, to be approved by the CISO]; and
b. Define system access authorizations to support separation of duties.

For AC-05, groups that must not be held together are listed in each group's `conflicts_with`. See POL-100 4.7.

### 3.3 AC-06 Least Privilege

Control: Employ the principle of least privilege, allowing only authorized accesses for users (or processes acting
on behalf of users) that are necessary to accomplish assigned organizational tasks.

(07) Least Privilege | Review of User Privileges.
  (a) Review [annually as part of the annual account review (per AC-02j)] the privileges assigned to [all roles and
  users] to validate the need for such privileges; and
  (b) Reassign or remove privileges, if necessary, to correctly reflect organizational mission and business needs.

For AC-06, where a role can be met by a group of lower sensitivity (for example, a dashboard *viewer* group rather
than the underlying data group), the lower one must be used. Data groups that grant direct access to underlying
tables are not required for viewing published dashboards.

### 3.4 AC-11 Device Lock

Control: Prevent further access to the system by initiating a device lock after [15 minutes] of inactivity.

### 3.5 AC-12 Session Termination

Control: Automatically terminate a user session after [30 minutes] of inactivity.

### 3.6 AC-17 Remote Access

Control: Establish and document usage restrictions, configuration/connection requirements, and implementation
guidance for each type of remote access allowed. Remote access from outside the country is covered by GUI-OVS.

### 3.7 AC-19 Access Control for Mobile Devices

Control: Establish configuration requirements, connection requirements, and implementation guidance for
company-controlled mobile devices. Lost or stolen mobile devices are handled under GUI-LOST. Mobile mail clients
that keep retrying an old password lock the account (see KB-1204).

## Appendix B: Policy

Note: When POL-100 is updated, the access control statements within it will supersede the policy statements below.

The following extracts from POL-100 (Revision P) contain information related to the implementation of access
control for company IT systems and data.

Chapter 4: Policy for Protection states:

1. Identity Management, Authentication and Access Control.

j. Information system user accounts (i.e., persons) must be managed for all systems, including establishing,
activating, modifying, reviewing, disabling, and removing user accounts. Company user account management processes
include:
  (1) Supervisors, contract sponsors, or account managers coordinating and arranging system access termination for
  all departing or resigning personnel, including both employees and contractors.
  (2) Supervisors, contract sponsors, or account managers initiating account removal, disablement, or permission
  changes based on a review of information provided by People Ops (e.g., separation lists, role revisions).
  (3) System Owners/account managers verifying that separated users no longer maintain access to company IT systems
  or resources after 30 days of separation.
  (4) Accounts disabled for inactivity may be re-enabled by the desk once the People team has confirmed the user's
  return under STD-ACP.
  (5) Supervisors or System/Data Owners submitting user access requests and user permission or role changes for
  account manager approval based on a user's job function and need-to-know.
  (6) A transferring user may retain access from their previous role for up to 30 days to complete a hand-over, with
  their previous supervisor's agreement.
