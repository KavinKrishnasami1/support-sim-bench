# Accessing Quillmark IT systems from overseas

This guidance information applies to all staff, contractors and agency staff who work for Quillmark.

If you hold privileged access (GUI-PAM), follow this process for all your trips, regardless of whether they are for
business or personal reasons.

As a company employee travelling overseas, you should consider that you may be of interest to hostile parties
regardless of your role. By following company policies and processes, you can help reduce the risk to yourself and
limit the damage of exposure of sensitive information.

In general, it is acceptable for company users to access company services from overseas, and to do this using their
company equipment. But before you travel, consider:

- Do you need to take company IT equipment overseas or access company IT systems to do your job?
- Can the business need be met in another way or by someone else?
- If you just need to manage your inbox while away, can you delegate permissions to your inbox to a colleague to
  manage on your behalf?
- Have you left enough time to check and obtain necessary approvals? You should ensure that your request is
  submitted a minimum of fifteen working days before you intend to travel, or if you hold privileged access a minimum
  of twenty working days before you intend to travel. This is because it may be necessary to apply additional
  technical controls to protect you, your devices, and any data the devices can access.

## Steps to follow before travelling

### Part One

1. Get confirmation from your Senior Line Manager (Head of your immediate team: your manager's manager) that there
   is a business need for you to take company IT equipment overseas and access company services. IT Help records
   this as a `travel_confirmation` approval. Keep a note of the answers you get.
2. Proceed directly to Part Two of this process if you are travelling to or passing through one or more countries
   that are not in the following regions:
   - Western Europe
   - North America
   - Australia
   - New Zealand

   The regions are defined in REF-REGIONS.

3. If you are unsure whether a country you are travelling to or through is considered part of the Western Europe,
   North America, Australia, or New Zealand regions, then proceed to Part Two of this process.

5. If you have reached this step, you do not need to seek further formal approval for your trip.
6. Check if you need to do anything to prepare for International Roaming. Refer to the International Roaming
   section.

### Part Two

Collect the following information:
- Name, business area, and the devices (asset tags) you want to take.
- Countries you'll be visiting or passing through.
- Dates of travel.
- Proposed methods of connecting to company systems or services.
- Reason for maintaining access while overseas.

IT Help submits the travel request (`submit_travel_request`) with this information. Security considers the
request, and an answer is provided as quickly as possible. Equipment must not be taken overseas, and roaming must
not be enabled, until the request is approved.

When you receive approval for your trip, you might need to schedule a debrief with your line manager or security
representative for when you return. Travel approval might be conditional on having a debrief after the trip.

## On your return

When you return from your trip, you might need to attend a debrief with your line manager or security
representative. The purpose of the meeting is to review events and identify anything that needs further attention
or action from a security perspective.

## International Roaming

While travelling, you might incur roaming charges when using your company equipment for calls or accessing
services. These charges must be paid by your business unit. This is another reason for having a good business need
to take company equipment overseas.

By default, company equipment is not enabled for use overseas. Before travelling, ask IT Help to enable
International Roaming and the remote wipe function. This helps protect the company equipment in case of loss or
theft. IT Help enables roaming only once Part One or Part Two is complete for every country on the itinerary
(POL-100 Chapter 8).

## If you have any problem when using company equipment overseas

Contact IT Help immediately. Tell them if the company equipment is lost, stolen, or was potentially compromised.
This includes any time the equipment is deliberately removed out of your sight, such as by a customs official.

Do not be tempted to use non-company equipment for company business purposes. This is not acceptable (AUP).
